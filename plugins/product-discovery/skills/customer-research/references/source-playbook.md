# Source selection and search

Use this as a menu. Choose sources because relevant customers describe experiences there; no platform or paid tool is mandatory. Verify current access through the tools available in the host.

## Match source families to the question

| Source family | Candidate sources | Useful evidence and limits |
|---|---|---|
| Software reviews | G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, specialist directories | Use cases, trade-offs, reasons to change. Read mixed, positive, and negative reviews; ratings do not establish honesty or authenticity. |
| Mobile reviews | App Store, Google Play | Version-specific experiences, subscriptions, failures, and ongoing use. Separate app versions and dates where possible. |
| Practitioner discussions | Reddit, Hacker News, Wykop, niche forums | Workflows, workarounds, alternatives, and firsthand accounts. Establish that the community fits the segment; replies and votes do not represent the market. |
| Professional/social posts | LinkedIn, X, public community posts | Adoption, switching, implementation, and daily use. Distinguish an actual practitioner account from promotion or a repost. |
| Video and comments | YouTube reviews, long-term usage reports, available social video comments | Experiences over time and discrepancies between promises and use. Attribute reviewer and commenter separately; disclose sponsorship when relevant. |
| Public product support | GitHub Issues/Discussions, user forums, public feedback boards | Specific failure cases and workarounds. Read status, resolutions, version information, and dates; requested features are not automatically unmet needs. |
| Consumer/local reviews | Google Maps, Ceneo, Opineo, relevant marketplaces | Product or service experience. Separate seller, shipping, and service complaints from product limitations. |

Customer stories hosted by a vendor are selected evidence. Use their actual customer statements only when relevant, label the context, and do not count vendor claims as opinions.

Private groups or paid communities are candidates only with actual authorized access or a supplied export. Do not promise to search closed Facebook groups, Slack, or Discord through public web search. Record inaccessible sources as gaps and use accessible alternatives.

## Build several search routes

Generate query variants in the target market's language. English and Polish results are separate evidence pools unless the question supports combining them. A source's language alone does not establish the author's location or market.

| Route | Query examples |
|---|---|
| Experience over time | `"[product]" "used for"`, `"[produkt]" "używam od"` |
| Positive value | `"[product]" "saved me"`, `"[produkt]" "polecam bo"` |
| Friction and trade-offs | `"[product]" "works but"`, `"[produkt]" "brakuje mi"` |
| Switching and leaving | `"[product]" "switched from"`, `"[produkt]" "zrezygnowałem"` |
| Alternatives | `"[product]" "alternative"`, `"[konkurent]" "zamiast"` |
| Task and workaround | `"[task]" "spreadsheet"`, `"[zadanie]" "robię ręcznie"` |
| Community-specific | `site:reddit.com "[problem]"`, `site:[forum-domain] "[zadanie]"` |

Examples are query patterns, not guaranteed platform syntax. Adapt to the search capability available. Use both search engines and native platform search when accessible; several query variants against one index are not several independent source families.

## Source-specific checks

Apply the evidence-verification rules in SKILL.md. Record publicly disclosed incentives or affiliations that affect independence.

## Adapt and stop

If results are mostly SEO lists, add community-specific queries and phrases people use to describe their work. Use roundups to discover product names, not as customer testimony.

If one platform dominates, try another relevant source family or record why coverage stayed narrow. If no reviews exist for the target product, competitor or category experiences may illuminate the problem, but label them as indirect evidence about the target. Never imply those authors used it.

Prefer recent material when product changes matter. Older accounts can establish historical context; they do not establish current behavior without a check. Do not apply a universal age cutoff to every category.

For a thin first pass, try native review feeds or exports alongside pagination and comment threads. Use the sample target, stopping criteria, deduplication, and mention-count rules in SKILL.md. Use accessible alternatives; never bypass access restrictions.
