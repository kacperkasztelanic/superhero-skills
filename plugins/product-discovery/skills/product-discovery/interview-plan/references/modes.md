# Interview modes

The skill picks the spine. The user is never asked to choose a methodology - they hired the skill to know the difference.

## Selection rule

| Decision at stake | Spine |
|---|---|
| Anything generative: what do people need, where does the current life hurt | **Story-based** (default) |
| Why did they leave, switch, buy, or cancel | **Switch timeline** |
| Testing a specific assumption behind a chosen solution | Story-based, narrowed to the moment that assumption lives in |

State the pick in one sentence with the reason, then move on.

## Story-based (default)

Teresa Torres's generative form. The goal of each interview is **at least one story about specific past behavior**. Needs, pains, and desires emerge from the story; you do not ask for them directly.

Why not ask directly: "what are your pain points?" gets an answer people are bad at giving, and "what do you need from a product like this?" gets feature requests, which puts the whole conversation in the solution space.

Shape:
1. Opening story prompt about a recent, concrete instance.
2. Excavate that story: sequence, people involved, what they tried, what they felt, what happened next.
3. Cover remaining topics only if the story left them untouched.
4. Close on the end state (facts, commitment, or advancement).

## Switch timeline

Bob Moesta and Chris Spiek's jobs-to-be-done interview, for decisions about leaving, switching, or buying. Reconstruct the timeline backwards from the switch:

**First thought → passive looking → active looking → deciding → consuming**

For each moment, ask what happened and what triggered the move to the next one. "When did you first think the old way was not working?" beats "why did you leave?".

Then read the four forces off the timeline:

| Force | Direction | What surfaces it |
|---|---|---|
| **Push** of the situation | toward change | "What was happening that made you start looking?" |
| **Pull** of the new solution | toward change | "What did you imagine would be better?" |
| **Anxiety** about the new | against change | "What, if anything, made you hesitate during the switch?" |
| **Habit** of the present | against change | "What kept you using the old way until then?" |

Push + pull beat anxiety + habit, or the switch does not happen. Most teams interview only for push and pull, then wonder why nobody adopts.

## Out of scope

Non-directed listening sessions (Indi Young) deliberately start without a hypothesis and follow the participant's inner reasoning rather than their behavior. That is a different craft with a different output, and it does not survive the disconfirmation gate this skill enforces. If the user's real question is "how do these people think", say so and point them at that method instead of bending this one.
