# Recruiting and the screener

The screener decides the quality of everything downstream. Most interview studies fail here, quietly, and the failure looks like "we talked to customers and learned nothing".

## Rules

1. **Ask about past behavior, not intentions.** "Which tools, if any, did you set up for your team in the last three months?" not "would you consider switching tools?".
2. **No yes/no questions.** They tell the candidate what you want, and people lie to get in. Use multiple choice with several plausible options and more than one disqualifying answer.

   The qualifying question is where this rule breaks in practice, because the thing you need to know really is a yes or a no. Turn it into a list instead: not "did you sign up for a new support tool in the last three months?" but "which of these did you do in the last three months? - hired an agent / changed your ticket process / signed up for a new tool / imported a knowledge base / none of these". Same filter, no signal about what you are looking for.
3. **Never reveal the purpose.** Funnel order: broadest questions first, specifics last. If the screener says what the study is about, the answers are already shaped.
4. **At least one open-ended question.** It catches professional participants and shows who answers in one word - which is exactly how they will answer in the interview.
5. **Screen only what a research question depends on.** Every extra demographic criterion shrinks the pool without improving the data.
6. **Plain language.** No internal jargon, no product names the candidate would not use.
7. **Two stages for high-stakes samples.** Written screener for the basics, then a 2-5 minute call to confirm role and context conversationally. Senior B2B participants can pass a written screener they should not have passed.

## Shape

```
1-2   broad context (role, category behavior)
3-5   qualifying behavior, past tense, multiple choice with real distractors
6     open-ended ("Describe how you handled X the last time")
7     research-participation history (screens out professionals)
8     logistics (availability, recording consent, accessibility needs)
```

## Channels and self-selection

People who answer fastest and volunteer most are their own segment. Name the channels in the plan, use more than one, and if that is impossible, write the bias down as a known limit of the study rather than discovering it during synthesis.

## Incentives

Pay fairly and pay promptly. Too little suppresses response, and late payment suppresses participation in later rounds. Where cash is impossible, Torres's exchange framing works: ask for twenty minutes and offer something concrete in return - early access, the summary of what you learn, a fix for the thing they are complaining about.

## IDs

Assign a pseudonym and an ID prefix per participant before the first conversation (A = Ana, B = Ben). Everything downstream - nuggets, quotes, traceability in `affinity-mapping` - runs on those IDs, and they double as pseudonymization for the consent story.
