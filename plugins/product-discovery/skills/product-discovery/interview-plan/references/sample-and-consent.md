# Sample size, cadence, consent

## How many

There is no universal number, and any skill that hands you one is lying.

- **~12 interviews** reached thematic saturation in Guest, Bunce and Johnson's study - on a homogeneous sample.
- **20-40** were needed when Hagaman and Wutich replicated it across multiple sites and cultures.
- Torres's answer is different in kind: interview **at least weekly**, because the habit matters more than the count.

"Five users" is a usability-testing rule of thumb. It does not apply to generative interviews. Never quote it here.

## What the plan writes down

Not a bare N. Three things:

- **Starting N** - 5-8 for one tight segment, 12+ when the segment is broad or splits into sub-groups.
- **Stopping rule** - "stop when three consecutive interviews produce no theme that is not already in the nuggets file".
- **Expansion rule** - "if interviews 4 and 5 contradict each other on the focus question, the segment is two segments; add 4 more and split the analysis".

## Session shape

45 minutes per participant: 20-30 interviewing, 15-25 writing the notes immediately after, while the story is still intact. Teams that batch note-writing for later lose the detail that made the interview worth having.

Two roles where possible: interviewer and notetaker. One person doing both talks more and listens less.

## Consent and data handling

Recording produces personal data. Three questions hide inside that sentence, and collapsing them into one is how plans get this wrong.

1. **Which rules apply, and on what lawful basis.** Consent is one basis among several, and for internal product research it is often not the best fit - a different basis may apply, and switching later is painful because consent can be withdrawn. Which rules apply depends on where the organization operates and who it addresses, not on the participant's location alone. This determination is not for the interviewer to improvise: record who made it and on what basis, and route it to whoever owns privacy or legal when participants span jurisdictions or the topic touches sensitive categories (health, beliefs, finances, trauma).
2. **Consent to record, where it is required.** Asked before recording starts, and granular: recording, transcription, internal sharing, and quoting outside the team are separate permissions, not one checkbox. Say it in the invitation rather than at the door - someone told at the top of the call agrees under pressure.
3. **Who else touches the data.** A transcription or AI note-taking vendor usually acts as a processor, which typically needs an agreement in place; check whether the organization's policy already covers the tool before introducing a new one mid-study.

Whatever the legal route, two things stay practical:

- **Withdrawal must be executable** - pseudonymized IDs, the mapping kept separately, and a way to delete one participant's data without unpicking the rest.
- **Storage** - a named place, restricted access, no raw recordings mailed around.

Put this in the plan as a checklist the team ticks before the first call, not as a paragraph nobody reads.
