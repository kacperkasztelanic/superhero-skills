# Interview catalog - complete options at each question

This catalog supplies possibilities, not a page template. For each unresolved stage show every item, adapting examples without dropping less recommended choices. Append **Custom answer / another option** and **Recommend for me based on the brief**. Do not require design theory. Present everything in the user's language, in ordinary chat text as specified in SKILL.md.

Ask one main question, give a reasoned recommendation, and accept numbers or descriptions. Explicitly identify multiple choice. Preserve each option's outcome and condition/cost; do not reduce it to an English style label.

Stages 1-5 define communication, 6-10 form/behavior, 11-12 feasibility. Respect answers supplied ahead of time without asking again. The interview establishes intent, not permission to lower execution quality. "Simple", "local", and "approachable" do not mean generic composition or missing agreed animations. After stage 12, synthesize independently; do not add a thirteenth concept-selection gate.

## Stage 1/12 - What are we creating, and what exists?

1. **New page for a real offer** - start from scratch; real offer facts are needed.
2. **Redesign an existing page** - preserve the real offer and necessary functions while changing composition; requires a URL or code.
3. **Polish an existing page** - limited changes within agreed scope; no unsolicited full redesign.
4. **Fictional business / creative demo** - the agent may invent concept and branding; demo content is not real evidence.

Ask which option and what the offer is; a few sentences or URL suffice. Ask the website language here if different from the conversation. Delegation does not authorize inventing a real offer: an unknown product is a material gap, not a graphic detail.

## Stage 2/12 - What type of offer must the page explain?

1. **B2B SaaS** - show improved work and its outcome; contextualize the interface.
2. **Developer tool / infrastructure** - show mechanism and integration; technical specifics must be true.
3. **Consumer app** - show an everyday situation and ease of use; avoid abstract benefits.
4. **Physical product / e-commerce** - show the object, details, and purchase terms; needs credible product assets.
5. **Service / consulting** - show outcome, scope, and collaboration; trust requires specifics.
6. **Studio / creative agency** - show work quality and fit; the portfolio must exist or be labeled illustrative.
7. **Portfolio / personal brand** - show a real person's skills and work; do not invent achievements.
8. **Course / program / community** - show participant change and its mechanism; no undocumented outcome promises.
9. **Event / launch** - show reasons to attend and practical facts; verify dates and availability.
10. **Place / travel / hotel / property** - show character and usefulness; imagery must not misrepresent the actual place.
11. **Marketplace / directory / job board** - show what can be found and compared; distinguish both sides' needs.
12. **Social initiative / campaign / fundraiser** - show the problem, credibility, and impact; organizational status and use of funds must be true.

Ask which model best describes the offer; for a hybrid choose a primary and optionally secondary model. Industry does not dictate style: finance need not mean navy blue.

## Stage 3/12 - Who visits, and what do they already understand?

1. **Unaware of the problem** - open with a recognizable situation; more context is needed.
2. **Aware of the problem, seeking a solution** - explain approach and outcome; avoid pitching too early.
3. **Comparing specific solutions** - show differences, limitations, and evidence; needs comparison arguments.
4. **Knows the brand/product and wants to act** - shorten the path to the CTA; a long introduction can obstruct it.
5. **Expert or buying-team evaluation** - needs detail and implementation risks; technical information must remain readable.
6. **Mixed traffic** - design one main path with additional explanations; identify the priority audience.

Ask which situation fits and who this person is in practice: what they want to do or find frustrating. Do not invent research or demographics; label a working hypothesis when evidence is missing.

## Stage 4/12 - What is the promise and primary action?

1. **Buy / order** - direct sale; price and terms must be available or clearly linked.
2. **Start / sign up / trial** - enter the product; needs a real registration path.
3. **Book a call / demo** - conversation before purchase; clarify expectations and next steps.
4. **Request a quote / contact** - needs-dependent offer; requires a working contact channel.
5. **Join a waitlist / newsletter** - capture interest; a working signup requires integration.
6. **Register for an event / program** - depends on dates, availability, and terms.
7. **Download / view a resource** - delivers a specific asset that must exist.
8. **Try an on-page demonstration** - experience value before proceeding; requires working demo logic.
9. **Support / sign / volunteer** - action for an initiative; needs a real endpoint and consequences.

Ask what the visitor should do and the most important promised outcome; collect a destination URL/contact channel if available. A secondary action may support the primary. Do not generate a price or numerical promise as a fact.

## Stage 5/12 - What can support the promise? [multiple choice]

1. **Real product / working demo** - experiential evidence; needs an available product or representative slice.
2. **Output sample / portfolio / before-and-after** - shows quality; needs publication rights and clear provenance.
3. **Data / results / research** - helps evaluate effects; requires source, scope, and context.
4. **Customer testimonials / references** - builds trust; only real and usable examples.
5. **Case study** - connects problem, work, and outcome; a logo alone is insufficient.
6. **Method / process / scope** - explains the approach; weaker outcome evidence than achieved results, but honest.
7. **Expertise / track record / certificates** - establishes preparation; no fabricated titles or partnerships.
8. **Price / terms / guarantee / limitations** - reduces uncertainty; must match the actual offer.
9. **No evidence yet** - propose a demo, method, or labeled example; no fabricated social proof.

Ask what is actually available and can be shown; accept numbers and assets. Delegation selects presentation, not facts. Distinguish desired evidence from assets that exist and were supplied. Collect materials here. Later record each selected type as available, to obtain from an authorized source, honestly substituted, or an unmet dependency. Do not promise sourcing and then skip it. Missing customer history does not lower visual ambition.

## Stage 6/12 - What should lead visually?

1. **Typography** - letterforms establish character, scale, and rhythm; copy and line breaks require particular care.
2. **Editorial photography** - framing, scale, and sequence create atmosphere; requires good, relevant photographs.
3. **Film / cinematic storytelling** - moving images lead the experience; higher media cost and a static variant required.
4. **Physical product as protagonist** - silhouette, materials, and details structure composition; requires faithful product representations.
5. **Software demonstration** - interface and output explain value; must not become a tiny illegible dashboard.
6. **Illustrated brand world** - original shapes and scenes explain the offer; needs a coherent illustration language.
7. **Diagrams / technical precision** - relationships organize complexity; decorative diagrams do not replace explanations.
8. **Data / information visualization** - comparisons and changes show value; use real or clearly labeled demo data.
9. **Gallery / quiet editorial** - selection, rhythm, and space expose quality; weak or insufficient assets undermine it.
10. **Spatial form / 3D** - objects and perspective explain form or systems; higher performance cost and a fallback needed.
11. **Graphic experiment / collage** - shapes, layers, and contrasts create character; needs rules to avoid randomness.

Ask which material should lead and optionally which one supports it. Do not choose two equal dominants; establish hierarchy in hybrids.

## Stage 7/12 - What personality should the brand have?

1. **Direct and precise** - clear communication and organized detail; avoid anonymity.
2. **Calm and refined** - restraint, selection, and space; does not automatically mean beige or luxury.
3. **Bold and expressive** - strong contrast, scale, and voice; requires readability control.
4. **Warm and human** - closeness, natural imagery, and plain language; not childishness.
5. **Playful and unconventional** - surprise and character; humor must not obstruct understanding.
6. **Raw and authentic** - directness, materiality, economical means; intention distinguishes it from unfinished work.
7. **Technical and forward-looking** - systems, precision, new possibilities; avoid an automatic neon "AI look".
8. **Existing brand identity** - work within its rules; needs materials and binding constraints.

Ask which personality fits, optionally a supporting trait and strong dislikes. Collect existing colors, fonts, and references here without a separate technical quiz.

## Stage 8/12 - How should composition be organized?

1. **Poster-like** - one strong dominant and major scale contrasts; discipline in content quantity.
2. **Editorial / magazine** - varied columns, crops, and captions; intentional mobile reordering required.
3. **Product showcase / scene** - an object or interface with explanation; the scene must explain the offer quickly.
4. **Chapter sequence** - successive scenes develop one thought; transitions must not block scrolling.
5. **Gallery / archive** - work or materials lead the narrative; maintain clear context and CTA.
6. **Modular system** - repeated rules enable comparison; avoid cards without a concrete purpose.
7. **Layered / collage** - overlap and depth create energy; harder mobile framing and readability.
8. **Minimal, one reading axis** - focus and a shorter path to action; character must come from refined proportions and content.

Ask which way of leading the eye fits. A primary rule may have one supporting rule; explain the opening and later rhythm it creates.

## Stage 9/12 - In what order should we persuade?

1. **Outcome -> mechanism -> proof -> action** - quick promise for problem-aware visitors; outcome must be credible.
2. **Situation/problem -> new approach -> offer -> action** - builds understanding; avoid a drawn-out introduction.
3. **Demonstration -> explanation -> terms -> action** - value appears early; the demo must be understandable without training.
4. **Story/case -> lessons -> offer -> action** - gives context and specifics; needs a real story or labeled example.
5. **Work/collection -> approach -> fit -> contact** - suits portfolios and studios; materials must carry the opening.
6. **Experience/place -> practical details -> booking** - atmosphere leads to a decision; essential facts cannot be hidden.
7. **Short offer -> proof -> direct action** - suits decided visitors; less space to educate new audiences.

Ask which path fits what visitors need to understand. This is argument logic, not a required section count or hero layout.

## Stage 10A/12 - How much motion?

1. **Deliberately static** - composition, imagery, and copy carry the page; preserve clear control states.
2. **Subtle** - microinteractions and short state changes; readability cannot depend on animation.
3. **Rhythmic** - layers, reframing/scaling, and linked transitions develop the narrative through natural scrolling; requires a directed sequence, not fade-ins alone.
4. **Expressive** - strong choreography of the principal material, depth/parallax, masks or transforms connect scenes; more work on fluidity, mobile, and reduced motion.
5. **Cinematic / spatial** - developed sequences of film, planes, framing, or space create continuity; highest media/testing cost, without scroll hijacking.

Ask which level; choose techniques and parameters yourself. Video and 3D are not rewards for higher levels. Explain that intensity concerns energy and experience, not quality or effort. Rhythmic can be restrained yet compositionally complex. Preserve previously agreed parallax, film, or transitions as requirements; an intensity label does not erase them.

## Stage 10B/12 - What moment or interaction should be memorable?

1. **Strong static composition** - distinctive framing, juxtaposition, or scale; a full choice without an extra gadget.
2. **Product demonstration** - a small action produces a visible result; requires real, limited logic.
3. **Comparison / before-and-after** - perspective change explains the difference; comparison must be honest.
4. **Detail/object exploration** - choosing a point/view reveals significance; provide touch and keyboard access.
5. **Simple calculator / configurator** - visitors test their scenario; calculation assumptions must be explicit.
6. **Scroll sequence** - successive states reveal a mechanism; content remains accessible without animation.
7. **Typography/image responding to action** - form responds to a choice or movement; must work without hover.
8. **Narrative surprise / change of scale** - the same material gains new meaning; no disorientation or hidden CTA.
9. **No separate showpiece** - a coherent, quiet experience; do not force memorability through effects.

Ask which moment strengthens the offer. Match 10A: static excludes mandatory automatic choreography, not functional controls. Do not mandate an interaction impossible to capture in a screenshot in every project. Option 1 describes one frame's strength, not a cancellation of page-wide motion from 10A. Option 9 removes a separate showpiece, not agreed sequences/transitions. For rhythmic and stronger motion, design a change whose meaning a single screenshot cannot convey; it may be integral to the narrative rather than an extra feature.

## Stage 11/12 - What visual sources can we use? [multiple choice]

1. **Owned photography / film / branding** - closest to the real brand; verify quality and rights.
2. **Real product / screenshots / outputs** - concrete offer; hide sensitive data and show a readable slice.
3. **Licensed asset library** - faster sourcing; verify fit and usage conditions.
4. **New illustrations / generated imagery** - original visual treatment; cannot impersonate clients, results, or the actual object.
5. **Code graphics / SVG / diagrams / charts** - precision and responsiveness; complex scenes have execution cost.
6. **Typography and composition without extra media** - fewer dependencies; text and detail must carry character.
7. **Assets will arrive later** - requires an honest, working fallback, not a fictional finished product.

Ask what exists and which sources are allowed; invite references and what the user likes in them. Record asset provenance and role. If this changes stage 6 feasibility, propose a concrete fallback.

## Stage 12/12 - Where should the result live?

1. **Working local website** - code and local preview without publication; default when no hosting was selected.
2. **Inside an existing project** - preserve stack, routing, and necessary functions; needs project location.
3. **New hosted website / Sites** - working URL through an available publishing workflow; establish audience rather than assuming public access.
4. **Existing hosted Site** - update the identified project while preserving identity/access; needs a URL or identifier.
5. **Detailed prompt and specification only** - no implementation at this stage; requires an explicit choice.

Ask where the finished result is wanted and invite repository/environment/binding technical requirements. Do not require a framework choice. Prefer simplicity and the existing stack. Hosting does not imply a domain purchase, analytics, or new integrations.

Local-only does not prohibit fonts, necessary libraries, animation, or real assets; it does not authorize replacing real functionality with a demo. Record technology and integration status separately with correct provenance.

## Contradictions and supplementary questions

During the interview, when a conflict needs the user's decision, show all actual resolutions and their consequences, plus a custom answer and delegation. Afterward resolve artistic/execution details independently. For "film with no assets and no sourcing budget", check permitted sources, production, or equivalent moving media. If the film cannot be obtained, record the unmet dependency; do not pretend completion or treat a static fallback as user consent to lower ambition.

A critical factual question may be open-ended; do not invent artificial choices for a price, date, address, or recipient. Offer real routes: provide the fact, cite a source, honestly omit it, or explicitly change scope to a demonstration. Delegation does not make an unknown fact true. After the interview apply independent synthesis and explicit dependency reporting without another questionnaire.
